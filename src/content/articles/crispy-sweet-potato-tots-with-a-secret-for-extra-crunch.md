<p>The first time I made sweet potato tots, they came out of the oven looking promising—golden, tiny, cute. But one bite told the truth: soft, a little soggy, more like a sad sweet potato pancake than a tot. I knew the problem was moisture, but simply roasting them longer only dried out the inside. So I went back to basics and found one simple trick that changed everything.</p>
<figure><img src="https://cdn.midjourney.com/875f8b9d-d69d-46f9-b6e6-bf53115be016/0_2.png" alt="Squeezing shredded sweet potatoes in a kitchen towel to remove excess moisture" /></figure>
<p>These sweet potato tots have a crisp shell that shatters slightly when you bite, giving way to a tender, almost creamy center. The sweetness of the potato is balanced by savory Parmesan and a whisper of smoked paprika. They're baked or air-fried, never deep-fried, so you get that crunch without the grease.</p><h2>The Secret: Squeeze, Don't Skip</h2><p>The single most important step in this recipe happens before anything else goes in the bowl. After grating the sweet potatoes, you must squeeze out as much liquid as humanly possible. Sweet potatoes hold a surprising amount of water, and that water is the enemy of crispiness.</p><p>I use a clean kitchen towel or a double layer of cheesecloth. Gather the shreds in the center, twist the cloth tightly, and wring it over the sink. You'll be amazed at how much orange-tinted liquid comes out. Keep squeezing until only a few drops emerge. This isn't a step you can rush or skip—it's the difference between tots that crunch and tots that slump.</p><h2>Why Parmesan Works Here</h2><p>Parmesan does more than add savory flavor. Its fine, dry texture helps absorb any remaining moisture from the sweet potato shreds, and it browns beautifully in the oven, creating a crisp outer crust. I use finely grated Parmesan from a block—the powdery stuff in a can won't give you the same texture or flavor.</p><p>If you need to keep this dairy-free, nutritional yeast adds a similar savory note, but you'll want to add a tablespoon of cornstarch to help with binding and crisping.</p><h2>Shaping and Cooking for Even Crunch</h2><p>Shape each tot firmly, packing the mixture together like a tiny snowball, then rolling it into a cylinder about an inch long and three-quarters of an inch wide. If the mixture feels too wet to hold its shape, stir in a tablespoon of cornstarch or all-purpose flour.</p><p>For oven-baked tots, 425°F is the sweet spot. That high heat drives off moisture quickly and encourages browning. Flip them halfway through so both sides get direct heat. For air fryers, 400°F works perfectly, and the circulating air gives you an even crunch in about half the time. Either way, let them rest for two minutes after cooking—this lets the exterior set fully so they don't crumble when you pick them up.</p><h2>Visual Cues for Doneness</h2><p>You're looking for a deep golden-brown color, with darker spots where the Parmesan has caramelized. The tots should feel firm when gently pressed, and the surface should look dry and slightly craggy. If they still look pale or feel soft, give them another 2–3 minutes. Underdone tots are the second most common mistake—don't pull them too early.</p><h2>Dipping and Serving Ideas</h2><p>These tots are excellent on their own, but a good dip takes them to party-ready status. Try a simple chipotle mayo (mayonnaise + adobo sauce from a can of chipotles), a tangy ranch, or even a maple-mustard combo. For a crowd, pile them on a platter with small bowls of two or three dips—they disappear fast.</p><h2>Takeaway</h2><p>The key to perfect sweet potato tots is removing moisture before cooking, using a binding ingredient that also crisps, and giving them enough heat and time to develop a crunchy shell. Squeeze well, shape firmly, and don't rush the oven. You'll get tots that are every bit as satisfying as the classic version—maybe more so.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<h3>Why are my sweet potato tots falling apart?</h3>
<p>The mixture likely has too much moisture or isn't packed firmly enough. Be sure to squeeze the shredded sweet potatoes very dry, and press each tot firmly into shape. If the mixture still seems wet, add 1 tablespoon of cornstarch or all-purpose flour to help bind it.</p>
<h3>Can I make sweet potato tots ahead of time?</h3>
<p>Yes. Shape the tots, place them on a parchment-lined tray, and refrigerate for up to 24 hours before cooking. You can also freeze them uncooked (see Notes). Bake or air fry straight from the fridge or freezer—just add a few extra minutes.</p>
<h3>What's the best way to reheat leftover sweet potato tots?</h3>
<p>Skip the microwave—it makes them soft. Instead, reheat in a 400°F oven or air fryer for 5–7 minutes until they're hot and crispy again. A toaster oven works well too.</p>
<h3>Can I use this recipe for other vegetables?</h3>
<p>Absolutely. This method works with grated zucchini (squeeze very dry), carrots, or parsnips. Adjust salt and spices to match the vegetable. For zucchini, add an extra tablespoon of cornstarch to absorb remaining moisture.</p>

		<ol><li>Preheat your oven to 425°F (220°C). If using an air fryer, preheat to 400°F (200°C).</li><li>Peel the sweet potatoes. Using a box grater, shred them on the large holes. Gather the corners and twist firmly over the sink to squeeze out as much liquid as possible. This is the secret to crispy tots—excess moisture makes them soggy.</li><li>In a large bowl, combine the squeezed sweet potato shreds, Parmesan, egg, chives, smoked paprika, garlic powder, salt, and pepper. Mix with your hands until everything is evenly coated.</li><li>Shape the mixture into small cylinders about 1 inch long and 3/4 inch wide. Pack each tot firmly so it holds together. Place them on a parchment-lined baking sheet or in the air fryer basket in a single layer, not touching.</li><li>For baking: Brush or drizzle the tots with oil, then bake for 20–25 minutes, flipping halfway through, until golden brown and crispy on all sides.</li><li>For air frying: Lightly spray the tots with cooking spray, then air fry for 12–15 minutes, shaking the basket halfway, until deep golden and crisp.</li><li>Let the tots rest on the baking sheet or basket for 2 minutes before serving. Sprinkle with a pinch of flaky salt if desired.</li></ol>	</div>
	<div class="wprm-fallback-recipe-notes">
		<p>Storage: Leftover tots keep in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 3 days. Reheat in a 400°F oven or air fryer for 5 minutes to restore crispness. Common allergens: This recipe contains dairy (Parmesan) and eggs. For a dairy-free version, omit the Parmesan and add 2 tablespoons of nutritional yeast and an extra tablespoon of cornstarch. For egg-free, substitute with 2 tablespoons of aquafaba or a flax egg (1 tablespoon ground flaxseed + 3 tablespoons water, rested for 5 minutes).
