<p>The first time I made watermelon feta salad, I was underwhelmed. The cubes of melon sat in a puddle of pink-tinged liquid, the feta had dissolved into a milky mess, and the mint wilted into sad green specks. I knew the combination was right — sweet, salty, herbaceous — but the technique was wrong.</p>
<figure><img src="https://cdn.midjourney.com/f9332722-b163-4b21-bdb0-420ed6d60869/0_2.png" alt="Close-up of cubed watermelon being salted in a bowl before adding other ingredients." /></figure>
<p>Here's the fix: salting the watermelon first. It's a small step that makes a big difference. The salt draws out excess water from the fruit, so when you add the dressing and cheese, the salad stays crisp, not soupy. The salt also heightens the melon's sweetness — a trick that works with any fruit.</p><p>This version uses a simple lime-honey dressing, but you can swap the lime for lemon or skip the honey entirely if your watermelon is very sweet. The ratio I keep is roughly 4 parts watermelon to 1 part feta, with enough mint to brighten every bite.</p><h2>The Right Watermelon</h2><p>Not all watermelons are equal for this salad. You want a seedless melon that's deep red and heavy for its size. The flesh should be firm, not mealy. If you can, buy a whole melon and cut it yourself — pre-cut cubes from the store are often older and more watery. A 2-pound wedge will give you about 4 cups of cubes.</p><p>Cut the watermelon into 1-inch cubes. Too small and they'll break apart when you toss the salad; too large and they're awkward to eat. Uniform cubes also mean even salting.</p><h2>Salting: The Technique</h2><p>Spread the cubes in a single layer on a sheet tray or in a large bowl. Sprinkle with kosher salt — I use about 1/2 teaspoon for 4 cups of melon. Toss gently, then let it rest for exactly 5 minutes. You'll see beads of moisture form on the surface, then pool at the bottom. Pour off that liquid. Do not rinse the melon. The light salt seasoning stays on the cubes, and the excess water is gone.</p><p>This step is non-negotiable if you want a salad that holds up for more than 10 minutes. Skip it, and you'll have watermelon soup.</p><h2>Choosing and Adding Feta</h2><p>Buy a block of feta, not pre-crumbled. Block feta has better texture and flavor — it's creamier, saltier, and less dry. Crumble it yourself into irregular pieces, or cut it into small cubes about the same size as the watermelon. Both work; cubes hold their shape a little longer, while crumbles blend into the dressing more.</p><p>Feta varies in saltiness. If yours is particularly salty, reduce or omit the extra salt on the watermelon. Taste the feta before you start.</p><h2>The Dressing: Simple and Balanced</h2><p>This dressing is barely a dressing — just oil, lime juice, a touch of honey, and black pepper. Whisk it in a small bowl until emulsified. The honey is optional; it rounds out the acidity of the lime. If your watermelon is very sweet, skip the honey. If it's a little underripe, the honey helps.</p><p>Pour the dressing over the watermelon and feta, then add the mint. Toss very gently with a large spoon or rubber spatula — you don't want to break the watermelon cubes. The feta will soften slightly into the dressing, creating a creamy coating on the melon. That's the texture you want.</p><h2>Mint: Fresh and Sliced</h2><p>Use fresh mint, not dried. Stack the leaves, roll them like a cigar, and slice crosswise into thin ribbons (chiffonade). This releases the mint's aroma without bruising the leaves. Add the mint just before serving so it stays bright green and perky.</p><p>If you don't have mint, basil works surprisingly well. Thai basil, with its hint of anise, is especially good.</p><h2>When to Serve</h2><p>This salad is best within 30 minutes of assembly. After that, even with the salting step, the watermelon will start releasing moisture. For a party, prep the components ahead — cut and salt the watermelon, crumble the feta, slice the mint, mix the dressing — then toss everything together just before guests arrive.</p><p>It's a natural pairing with grilled chicken, fish, or burgers. On a hot day, it's also a light lunch on its own.</p><h2>Common Mistakes to Avoid</h2><ul><li><strong>Skipping the salt step.</strong> This is the most common error. Without it, the salad turns watery within minutes.</li><li><strong>Using pre-crumbled feta.</strong> It's drier and less flavorful. Block feta is worth the extra 30 seconds to crumble.</li><li><strong>Overdressing.</strong> You don't need much — 2 tablespoons of oil and 1 of lime is enough for 4 cups of melon. Too much dressing makes the salad soggy.</li><li><strong>Adding mint too early.</strong> Mint wilts quickly. Add it right before serving for the best color and flavor.</li></ul><p><strong>Takeaway:</strong> The key to a great watermelon feta salad is salting the fruit first. It's a simple technique that prevents a watery mess and concentrates the melon's sweetness. With a 5-minute rest and a light hand with the dressing, you'll have a summer salad that's crisp, balanced, and genuinely refreshing.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<h3>Why do you salt the watermelon before adding the dressing?</h3>
<p>Salting draws out excess moisture from the watermelon. This prevents the salad from becoming watery and diluted after you add the dressing and feta. It also lightly seasons the fruit, making the sweet-salty contrast more pronounced. Let it sit for 5 minutes, then pour off the liquid — don't rinse.</p>
<h3>Can I use a different cheese instead of feta?</h3>
<p>Yes. A mild goat cheese or queso fresco works well. Avoid very soft cheeses like mozzarella or ricotta, as they'll blend in too much and lack the salty tang that balances the sweet watermelon. For a dairy-free option, use a firm, salted vegan feta.</p>
<h3>What's the best watermelon for this salad?</h3>
<p>Seedless watermelon is easiest, but any ripe, sweet melon works. Look for one that feels heavy for its size and has a creamy yellow spot on the rind (where it rested on the ground). The flesh should be deep red — paler melons are less sweet and will make a watery salad.</p>
<h3>Can I add other ingredients like cucumber or red onion?</h3>
<p>Absolutely. Thinly sliced cucumber (English or Persian, skin on) adds crunch. A few thin slices of red onion, soaked in cold water for 5 minutes to tame the bite, work too. Just keep the ratio roughly the same — don't overload the salad with extras, or the watermelon and feta will get lost.</p>
