<p>The first thing you notice is the sizzle. That bright, sharp hiss as the salmon hits the hot grates. Then the smell — garlic blooming in the heat, lemon cutting through the smoke. A good grilled lemon garlic salmon should have a crisp, charred exterior and a center that's just barely flaking apart. Not dry. Not raw. Just right.</p>
<figure><img src="https://cdn.midjourney.com/a41fbf19-6512-40ea-b2ba-82661d32985a/0_3.png" alt="Salmon fillets marinating in a glass dish with lemon garlic mixture" /></figure>
<p>I've grilled a lot of salmon over the years, and the difference between okay and outstanding comes down to two things: the marinade ratio and the grill temperature. Let me walk you through both.</p><h2>The Lemon Garlic Marinade: Getting the Balance Right</h2><p>Most marinades for salmon are either too acidic (which cooks the fish before it hits the grill) or too oily (which just slides off). I keep the ratio at <strong>3 parts oil to 1 part acid</strong>. For this recipe, that's 3 tablespoons olive oil to 1 tablespoon lemon juice. The remaining tablespoon of lemon juice goes on after grilling, so you get that bright pop without the fish turning mushy.</p><p>The garlic? Minced fine — not pressed into a paste. You want little bits that toast on the grill, not burn. And a half teaspoon of Dijon mustard does something subtle but essential: it helps the marinade cling to the fish and adds a gentle tang that plays off the lemon.</p><h2>Choosing and Prepping the Salmon</h2><p>Look for fillets that are roughly the same thickness — about 1 inch at the thickest part. Skin-on is best for grilling. The skin protects the delicate flesh from direct heat and makes flipping much easier. If your fillets have pin bones, run your fingers along the surface and pull them out with tweezers.</p><p>Pat the fillets dry. This is non-negotiable. Wet salmon steams instead of searing, and you'll lose those beautiful grill marks. Let them sit at room temperature for 10 minutes while you make the marinade. Cold fish on a hot grill cooks unevenly.</p><h2>The Grill Setup: Medium-High and Ready</h2><p>Preheat your grill to <strong>400°F (medium-high)</strong>. If you're using charcoal, the coals should be covered with a light gray ash. Clean the grates thoroughly with a wire brush, then oil them. I fold a paper towel, dip it in vegetable oil, and use tongs to rub it over the grates. Don't use olive oil here — it burns too quickly.</p><p>You want the grill hot enough to sear but not so hot that the sugar in the marinade burns. At 400°F, you get a golden char in about 4-5 minutes per side.</p><h2>Grilling: The Technique That Works Every Time</h2><p>Place the salmon <strong>skin-side down</strong> on the grill. Close the lid. Then do nothing for 4 minutes. No peeking, no poking. The fish needs that undisturbed contact to develop a crust and release naturally from the grates.</p><p>After 4 minutes, gently lift the edge of a fillet with a thin metal spatula. If it releases easily, it's ready to flip. If it sticks, give it another 30-60 seconds. Flip carefully — the skin should be crisp and browned.</p><p>Cook the second side for <strong>3-4 minutes</strong> for medium (the center will be just slightly translucent). For well-done, go 5 minutes. The internal temperature should reach <strong>145°F</strong> at the thickest part. The flesh should flake when you press it with a fork and look opaque all the way through.</p><h2>Common Mistakes and How to Avoid Them</h2><ul><li><strong>Over-marinating:</strong> More than 30 minutes in an acidic marinade turns the fish mushy. Set a timer.</li><li><strong>Moving the fish too soon:</strong> It will stick. Wait for it to release naturally.</li><li><strong>Grilling on too high heat:</strong> The outside burns before the inside cooks. Stick to 400°F.</li><li><strong>Skipping the rest:</strong> Let the salmon rest 2 minutes off the grill. The juices redistribute, and the carryover heat finishes the cooking gently.</li></ul><p>One more thing: if you're worried about sticking, you can use a piece of heavy-duty aluminum foil lightly oiled. Poke a few holes in it for smoke circulation. You won't get as many grill marks, but the flavor will still be excellent.</p><h2>Takeaway</h2><p>Grilled lemon garlic salmon is one of those dishes that looks impressive but comes together in under 30 minutes. The key is respecting the fish: don't over-marinade, don't crowd the grill, and don't rush the flip. Get those basics right, and you'll have a dinner that's bright, smoky, and satisfying every time.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<h3>What internal temperature should grilled salmon reach?</h3>
<p>Cook salmon to an internal temperature of 145°F, measured at the thickest part with an instant-read thermometer. The flesh should be opaque and flake easily with a fork.</p>
<h3>How do I keep salmon from sticking to the grill?</h3>
<p>Clean and oil the grill grates thoroughly just before adding the salmon. Start with a hot grill (400°F), and place the salmon skin-side down. Don't move it for the first 4-5 minutes — it will release naturally when it's ready to flip.</p>
<h3>Can I use frozen salmon for this recipe?</h3>
<p>Yes, but thaw it completely in the refrigerator overnight first. Pat very dry with paper towels before marinating. Frozen salmon releases more moisture, so you may need an extra minute of cook time per side.</p>
<h3>How long should I marinate salmon?</h3>
<p>15 minutes at room temperature is ideal. The acid in the lemon juice will start to cook the fish if left longer than 30 minutes, making it mushy. If you need more time, skip the lemon juice in the marinade and add fresh lemon after grilling.</p>
