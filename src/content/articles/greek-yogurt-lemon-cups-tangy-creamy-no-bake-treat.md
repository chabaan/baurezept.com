<p>The first spoonful hits you with a bright, sharp lemon tang—then gives way to a cool, creamy richness that lingers just long enough. These Greek Yogurt Lemon Cups are exactly that: a crisp, buttery graham cracker crust holding a cloud-light yogurt filling, topped with a glossy ribbon of homemade lemon curd. No oven required, no complicated steps. Just a few bowls and a little patience while they chill.</p>

<h2>Why Greek Yogurt? The Method Behind the Creaminess</h2>
<p>Greek yogurt brings a thick, almost mousse-like texture to the filling without needing gelatin or heavy cream. The key is to use full-fat or 2% plain Greek yogurt—nonfat will work, but it yields a thinner, slightly more tart result. I always let the cream cheese come to room temperature before mixing; otherwise, you'll end up with small lumps that won't whisk out. The ratio I keep is 1 cup yogurt to 1/4 cup cream cheese—this gives enough structure to hold its shape while staying light on the tongue.</p>

<h2>The Crust: A No-Bake Foundation</h2>
<p>A good crust needs to stay crisp against the moist filling. I use graham cracker crumbs mixed with melted butter and just a tablespoon of sugar. The trick is pressing the crumbs firmly into the bottom of the ramekin—use the flat bottom of a small glass or a measuring cup to compact them. If they're too loose, they'll crumble when you eat the cup. For an even cleaner edge, line the ramekin with a parchment paper circle before pressing the crust; lift it out just before serving.</p>

<h2>Making the Lemon Curd: Getting the Temperature Right</h2>
<p>Homemade lemon curd is surprisingly easy, but heat control matters. Whisk the egg, sugar, lemon juice, and zest together in a small saucepan before turning on the heat. Then cook over <strong>medium-low</strong>, whisking constantly. You'll see the mixture go from thin and frothy to thick and glossy—when it coats the back of a spoon and leaves a clear trail when you run your finger through it, it's done. That usually takes 4 to 5 minutes. If you see even one bubble, pull the pan off the heat immediately; boiling will scramble the egg. The cold butter cubes stirred in at the end add shine and a velvety finish. Strain through a fine-mesh sieve to catch any stray cooked bits, and you'll have a smooth, vibrant curd.</p>

<h2>Assembling the Cups</h2>
<p>Chill the crusts for at least 10 minutes before adding the filling. Spoon the yogurt mixture gently to avoid disturbing the crust, then smooth the tops. The filling needs a solid 2 hours in the fridge to set—overnight is even better. Once set, add a thin layer of lemon curd (about 1 tablespoon per cup) and chill for another 30 minutes. This allows the curd to firm up slightly, so it doesn't run off the sides when you serve.</p>

<h3>Common Mistake to Avoid</h3>
<p>Don't skip the chilling steps. If you add the curd to a soft filling, it will sink in and make the top messy. Patience here pays off in clean layers.</p>

<h2>Storage and Serving Tips</h2>
<p>These cups keep well in the refrigerator, covered, for up to 3 days. The crust will soften a little over time, but the flavor stays bright. For a crunchier crust, serve them the same day you make them. If you're prepping for a party, assemble the cups (without the curd) up to 2 days ahead, then add the curd a few hours before serving.</p>
<p><strong>Allergen note:</strong> This recipe contains dairy (yogurt, cream cheese, butter) and eggs. For a dairy-free version, use a thick plant-based yogurt (like coconut or almond) and dairy-free cream cheese. For a gluten-free crust, substitute gluten-free graham crackers or crushed almonds. For any dietary concerns, please consult a qualified professional.</p>

<h2>The Takeaway</h2>
<p>These Greek Yogurt Lemon Cups are the kind of dessert that feels special but takes almost no active work. The contrast of textures—crisp, creamy, silky—keeps every bite interesting. And that first hit of lemon? It's exactly what a bright, refreshing treat should be.</p>
