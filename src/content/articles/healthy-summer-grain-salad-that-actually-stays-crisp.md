<p>There's a moment every summer when the kitchen gets too hot for anything that requires the oven. That's when I turn to a grain salad that's been a reliable workhorse for years. The trick is in the cooling—spreading the cooked grains on a baking sheet to stop them from steaming into a clumpy mess. It's a small step, but it makes the difference between a salad that stays light and one that turns into a sticky bowl of mush.</p>

<h2>Why Farro Works Best</h2>
<p>Farro is my go-to for this salad because it holds its shape after cooking and has a pleasant chew that contrasts with the soft tomatoes and crisp cucumber. Semi-pearled farro cooks in about 25 minutes, and it doesn't turn gummy like some other grains can. If you want a gluten-free option, quinoa or brown rice work well—just adjust the cooking liquid and time accordingly.</p>

<h2>The One Mistake to Avoid</h2>
<p>Most grain salads go wrong because the grains are still warm when the dressing goes on. Warm grains absorb the dressing unevenly, leaving some parts dry and others soggy. I spread the cooked farro in a single layer on a baking sheet and let it cool for a full 10 minutes. Fluff it with a fork once it's cool to the touch. This simple step ensures every grain gets an even coating later.</p>

<h2>Vegetable Prep: The Little Things</h2>
<p>Seeding the cucumber is non-negotiable here. The watery center of a cucumber will dilute the dressing and make the salad weep within a few hours. Halve the cucumber lengthwise and run a small spoon down the center to scoop out the seeds. Then dice into half-inch pieces. Cherry tomatoes should be halved, and red onion sliced paper-thin—a mandoline helps if you have one, but a sharp knife works just fine.</p>

<h2>The Dressing: Bright and Simple</h2>
<p>A lemon vinaigrette is all this salad needs. I whisk together olive oil, fresh lemon juice, a tiny bit of minced garlic, salt, and pepper. The ratio is roughly 3 parts oil to 2 parts lemon juice, which keeps the dressing tangy without being harsh. If you're making this ahead, keep the dressing separate and toss just before serving to keep the vegetables crisp.</p>

<h2>Assembling and Serving</h2>
<p>Combine the cooled farro, prepared vegetables, and fresh herbs in a large bowl. Pour the dressing over and toss gently—I use my hands to make sure everything is evenly coated without crushing the tomatoes. If you're adding feta and pine nuts, fold them in at the last minute so they stay distinct. Serve at room temperature or cold; it's equally good either way.</p>

<h2>Make-Ahead and Storage Tips</h2>
<p>This salad keeps in the fridge for up to four days. The flavors actually improve after a day as the herbs and lemon meld with the grains. If you're meal-prepping, store the dressing separately and add feta and nuts only to the portions you're eating that day. For a quick lunch, scoop it straight from the container—it needs no reheating.</p>

<h2>Variations to Try</h2>
<p>Swap the farro for cooked quinoa for a lighter, gluten-free version. Add diced bell peppers, shredded carrots, or blanched green beans for more crunch. For a protein boost, toss in canned chickpeas or grilled chicken. The lemon-herb dressing pairs well with almost any combination of vegetables and grains.</p>

<h2>Takeaway</h2>
<p>A great grain salad comes down to technique: cool your grains completely, seed your cucumber, and dress just before serving. Follow those three rules, and you'll have a salad that stays crisp, flavorful, and satisfying all week long.</p>
