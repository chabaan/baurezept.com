<p>The first time I made nachos at home, I ended up with a sad pile of soggy chips and unmelted cheese. The problem was simple: I poured the salsa and hoped for the best. After testing batch after batch, I landed on a technique that gives every chip a fair share of toppings — and keeps them crunchy. These Loaded Salsa Nachos start with a simple ratio: one part salsa to two parts cheese, spread over a single layer of chips. The result is a platter of evenly coated, melty, crispy bites that disappear fast at any gathering.</p>

<h2>The Secret to Even Topping Distribution</h2>
<p>The biggest mistake with nachos is piling everything in the center. The chips on the edges get nothing, and the ones in the middle turn to mush. I spread the tortilla chips in a single layer on a rimmed baking sheet. A 13-ounce bag of restaurant-style chips (about 8 cups) fits perfectly on a standard half-sheet pan. Overlap is minimal — just enough to hold toppings, but not so much that chips steam each other.</p>

<p>For the salsa, I use a spoon to dollop small teaspoon-sized amounts over the chips. I aim for one dollop per cluster of 3–4 chips. This keeps the salsa concentrated where it matters, without flooding the chips. The rest of the moisture comes from the beans and corn, which I drain thoroughly before scattering. A colander is your best friend here — pat the beans and corn dry with a paper towel if they still look wet.</p>

<h2>The Cheese Blend Matters</h2>
<p>I use a 50-50 mix of Monterey Jack and sharp cheddar. Monterey Jack melts into a creamy, stretchy layer, while cheddar adds sharpness and color. Shredding from a block gives a cleaner melt than pre-shredded bags, which contain starch that can make the cheese grainy. I shred about 3 ounces of each cheese — 1½ cups total — which covers the chips evenly without overwhelming them.</p>

<p>Sprinkle the cheese from a height of about 6 inches, letting it fall in a light, even shower. Make sure to reach the edge chips — they need cheese too. The pickled jalapeños go on next, spaced out so each bite can find one if desired.</p>

<h2>Baking for the Perfect Crunch</h2>
<p>A 400°F oven is the sweet spot. It melts the cheese in 5–7 minutes without burning the chips. I place the baking sheet on the middle rack and watch closely after 4 minutes. The visual cue is the cheese — it should be fully melted with a few small bubbles, and the edges of the chips should just begin to deepen in color. Pull them out the moment you see that browning. Over-baking makes the chips taste scorched and the cheese greasy.</p>

<p>Let the nachos rest on the baking sheet for 2 full minutes after they come out of the oven. This brief rest sets the cheese so it clings to the chips instead of sliding off when you lift one. It also allows any residual steam to escape, keeping the base chips crisp.</p>

<h2>Toppings That Finish the Dish</h2>
<p>Cold toppings go on after baking to preserve their texture. I dollop sour cream in small spoonfuls — about 2 teaspoons each — spaced across the surface. Diced avocado adds creaminess and color; use a ripe avocado that yields slightly to gentle pressure. Fresh cilantro and a squeeze of lime juice brighten the whole platter. Serve immediately while the cheese is still warm and stretchy.</p>

<h3>Pro Tip for a Bigger Batch</h3>
<p>If you're feeding a crowd, make two separate baking sheets rather than one overloaded pan. Stacking chips or piling on too many toppings leads to uneven cooking and sogginess. Two sheets baked back-to-back (or on separate racks, rotated halfway) give you twice the nachos without sacrificing quality.</p>

<h2>A Note on Allergens and Substitutions</h2>
<p>This recipe contains dairy and corn. For a dairy-free version, use a plant-based shredded cheese that melts well — check the package for meltability ratings. The black beans and corn are naturally gluten-free, but verify your tortilla chip label if you have celiac concerns. For specific dietary needs, consult a qualified professional.</p>

<p><strong>Takeaway:</strong> The best nachos come from a single layer of chips, dolloped salsa, and a hot oven. Respect the ratio, watch the clock, and you'll never settle for a sad pile again.</p>
