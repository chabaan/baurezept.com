<p>There's something about the contrast — a warm, fudgy brownie meeting a scoop of cold vanilla ice cream. The brownie should be dense and chewy, not cakey. The ice cream should be firm, not soupy. And the sauce? It needs to pour like warm syrup, not seize up or turn grainy. Most recipes get one of those elements wrong. Here's how to get all three right.</p>

<h2>The Brownie: Fudgy, Not Cakey</h2>
<p>The key to a brownie that holds up under ice cream is the fat-to-flour ratio. I keep the ratio at 1 part butter to 1 part sugar to 1/2 part flour by volume. That means for every cup of sugar, you use half a cup of flour. No more. That's what gives you a dense, fudgy crumb that doesn't crumble apart when you press a scoop of ice cream on top.</p>
<p>Melt the butter first — don't cream it with sugar. That's the difference between a brownie and a cake. When you melt butter, it coats the flour proteins and limits gluten formation. The result is a tender, almost gooey interior. Whisk the eggs in one at a time until the mixture looks glossy and thick. That gloss means you've incorporated enough air for structure, but not so much that it rises into a cake.</p>
<p>Bake at 350°F (175°C) in an 8x8-inch pan lined with parchment. The parchment overhang lets you lift the whole slab out cleanly. Check for doneness at 22 minutes: a toothpick inserted in the center should come out with a few moist crumbs, not wet batter. The edges will be set and the center will still jiggle slightly. That's exactly what you want. Let them cool completely — at least 30 minutes — before cutting. Warm brownies will melt the ice cream on contact, turning your sundae into a puddle.</p>

<h2>The Sauce: Glossy and Pourable</h2>
<p>Most hot fudge sauces harden on contact with cold ice cream. That's fine for a dip, but for a sundae, you want a sauce that stays soft and pourable. The trick is a combination of heavy cream and corn syrup. The cream provides richness; the corn syrup prevents sugar crystals from forming, which keeps the sauce smooth even after it hits the cold ice cream.</p>
<p>Use a 1:1 ratio of heavy cream to chocolate chips by volume. For every half cup of chips, use half a cup of cream. Add one tablespoon of corn syrup per half cup of cream. Heat everything together over low heat, stirring constantly with a rubber spatula. Never let it boil — bubbles mean the water in the cream is evaporating, which can cause the sauce to seize later. When the sauce is smooth and warm, remove it from the heat. It should coat the back of a spoon but still drip freely.</p>
<p>Pour the sauce just before serving. If you let it sit on the ice cream too long, the cold will thicken it. A slow, steady pour over the center of the scoop gives you that glossy cascade.</p>

<h2>Assembly: The Right Order</h2>
<p>Start with a cooled brownie square at the bottom of a wide bowl or a shallow dish. A wide bowl gives you room for toppings without everything sliding off. Place one generous scoop of vanilla ice cream in the center. Press it gently so it adheres to the brownie, but don't smash it.</p>
<p>Pour the warm sauce over the ice cream, not the brownie. The sauce will flow down the sides of the ice cream and pool around the brownie, but the brownie itself stays dry and firm. That's the technique that prevents sogginess. If you pour sauce directly on the brownie, it soaks in and turns mushy within minutes.</p>
<p>Add whipped cream if you like, but keep it light — a dollop, not a mountain. A cherry on top is traditional, but optional. Serve immediately. This sundae doesn't wait.</p>

<h3>Common Mistake: Overloading the Sundae</h3>
<p>It's tempting to pile on every topping in the pantry. But too many wet ingredients — syrups, sauces, fresh fruit — will overwhelm the brownie and make everything slide apart. Stick to two or three toppings maximum. The brownie and ice cream are the stars. Everything else is a supporting player.</p>

<h2>Variations Without Losing the Technique</h2>
<p>You can swap the vanilla ice cream for coffee, chocolate, or salted caramel. The same sauce works with dark chocolate or milk chocolate chips. For a nutty crunch, add toasted pecans or walnuts on top of the sauce, not under it, so they stay crisp.</p>
<p>If you're serving a crowd, set up a sundae bar with a platter of brownie squares, a tub of ice cream, and a pitcher of warm sauce. Let everyone assemble their own. Just remind them: brownie first, then ice cream, then sauce. The order matters.</p>

<h2>Takeaway</h2>
<p>A brownie ice cream sundae is simple, but simple doesn't mean sloppy. Get the brownie fudgy, the sauce glossy, and the assembly order right, and you'll have a dessert that looks as good as it tastes. No sogginess, no graininess, no disappointment. Just a cold scoop on a warm brownie with sauce that pours like it means it.</p>
