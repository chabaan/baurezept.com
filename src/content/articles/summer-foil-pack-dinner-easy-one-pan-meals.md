<p>There's a moment in summer when the kitchen feels too warm, and the idea of turning on the oven makes you wilt. That's when I reach for a roll of heavy-duty aluminum foil. A summer foil-pack dinner is the solution: everything cooks together in its own little pouch, and cleanup is practically nonexistent. The chicken comes out juicy, the vegetables tender but still bright, and the lemon-herb butter sauce ties it all together.</p>

<h2>Why Foil Packets Work So Well</h2>
<p>The magic is in the steam. When you seal the foil tightly, the heat creates a moist environment that cooks the chicken gently and evenly. The vegetables soften without turning to mush—as long as you cut them the right size. I keep the pieces at about 1/2-inch thick. That way, they cook at the same rate as the chicken. Thicker cuts stay too crunchy; thinner ones disintegrate.</p>

<h2>Choosing the Right Ingredients</h2>
<p>Boneless, skinless chicken breasts are my go-to here. They cook quickly and stay moist inside the packet. If you prefer thighs, go ahead—just add a couple of minutes to the cook time. For vegetables, zucchini and bell peppers are naturals. They hold up well and soak up the butter sauce. Red onion adds a mild sweetness. You can swap in asparagus, cherry tomatoes, or mushrooms, but keep the total volume similar. Overstuffing the packet means uneven cooking.</p>

<h3>The Butter Sauce Ratio</h3>
<p>This is the part that makes the dish sing. I use 4 tablespoons of melted butter to 2 tablespoons of lemon juice, plus garlic and oregano. That's a 2:1 butter-to-lemon ratio. It's enough to coat the chicken and vegetables without making them greasy. If you want a lighter option, substitute olive oil for half the butter. The flavor shifts but still works beautifully.</p>

<h2>How to Assemble a Perfect Foil Packet</h2>
<p>Start with a 12x18-inch sheet of heavy-duty foil. Lightly spray the center with cooking spray—this prevents the chicken from sticking. Place the chicken in the middle, then arrange the vegetables around it. Drizzle the butter mixture evenly over everything. Don't skip the fresh thyme sprig; it adds a subtle earthiness that complements the lemon.</p>
<p>To seal, bring the long sides together over the food and fold down twice in tight, flat folds. Then fold the short ends in toward the center, pressing firmly. Leave a little air space inside—about an inch—so steam can circulate. A tight seal is crucial. If steam escapes, the chicken dries out.</p>

<h2>Cooking: Grill or Oven</h2>
<p>On a preheated grill at medium-high heat (around 400°F), the packets cook in 18 to 20 minutes. In the oven at 425°F, the same time applies. The most reliable doneness cue is temperature: insert an instant-read thermometer through the foil into the thickest part of the chicken. It should read 165°F. The vegetables should be tender when pierced with a fork but still hold their shape.</p>
<p>Common mistake: opening the packet too early. Let it rest for two minutes after cooking. That brief rest lets the juices redistribute. When you cut the foil open, do it carefully—the steam is hot enough to burn. Use a knife to slit the top and peel back the edges.</p>

<h2>Variations to Try</h2>
<p>This base recipe welcomes changes. Swap the chicken for salmon fillets (cook for 12-15 minutes at 400°F). Or use sliced Italian sausage for a heartier meal. For a vegetarian version, double the vegetables and add a can of drained chickpeas. Adjust the seasoning accordingly—smoked paprika or cumin work well with chickpeas.</p>

<h2>Serving and Storing</h2>
<p>Serve the packets directly at the table—everyone opens their own. It's a casual, fun way to eat. Garnish with fresh parsley for a pop of color. Leftovers keep in the fridge for up to three days. Reheat gently in the microwave or oven. The foil packets can be prepared a few hours ahead and kept in the fridge; just add a minute or two to the cooking time.</p>

<h2>A Quick Takeaway</h2>
<p>Foil-pack dinners are about efficiency without sacrificing flavor. The sealed packet does the work, so you don't have to. With a little attention to the seal and the cook time, you get a complete meal that tastes like summer. And the best part? No pile of dishes afterward.</p>
