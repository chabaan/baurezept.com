<p>The first time I made a drip cake, the ganache ran straight off the sides and pooled on the plate like a sad chocolate puddle. That's when I learned the secret: temperature. A drip-decorated ice cream cake is all about timing and technique. Get those right, and you'll have a glossy, dramatic dessert that looks like it came from a bakery — but tastes even better because you made it.</p>

<h2>Why This Technique Works</h2>
<p>The key to a perfect drip is a ganache that's thick enough to cling but fluid enough to fall. I keep the ratio at 1:1 cream to chocolate by weight, with a touch of corn syrup for shine and flexibility. The corn syrup also prevents the ganache from cracking when it hits the frozen cake. The cake itself needs to be fully frozen — at least 4 hours, preferably overnight — so the drip sets on contact without soaking in.</p>

<h2>Building the Layers</h2>
<p>Start with a sturdy base. A chocolate cookie crust provides structure and flavor. Press it firmly into the pan — I use the bottom of a measuring cup for even pressure. Freeze it for 15 minutes before adding the first ice cream layer.</p>
<p>For the ice cream, use good-quality brands with higher fat content (14-16% milkfat). Lower-fat ice creams contain more air and water, which creates icy crystals and a softer cake. Let the ice cream soften in the fridge for 20 minutes, not on the counter. This slows the melting and keeps layers distinct.</p>
<p>Spread the vanilla layer first, smoothing it with an offset spatula. Freeze for 2 hours minimum. Then add the chocolate layer. The two-hour freeze between layers prevents them from blending into a muddy swirl. When you spread the second layer, work from the center outward to avoid disturbing the first layer.</p>

<h2>The Drip: Temperature Is Everything</h2>
<p>Make the ganache while the cake gets its final freeze. Heat the cream until it's steaming but not boiling — about 180°F (82°C) on a thermometer. Pour it over the chocolate chips, let sit for 2 minutes, then stir gently until smooth. Add the corn syrup and salt. Let it cool to 90°F (32°C). This is the sweet spot.</p>
<p>Test the consistency on the back of a cold spoon: if it runs off in a steady stream, it's ready. If it's too thick, add a teaspoon of warm cream. Too thin, let it cool another minute. Pour the ganache from a height of about 2 inches above the center of the cake. Let it pool, then gently tilt the plate so the drip runs evenly around the sides. Work quickly — the cake is cold and the ganache will start setting within 30 seconds.</p>

<h2>Garnish and Slice</h2>
<p>Before the ganache fully sets, sprinkle your topping around the edge. Toasted almonds add crunch and contrast. Or use colorful sprinkles for a birthday vibe. Freeze the cake for 30 minutes to lock everything in place.</p>
<p>For clean slices, use a chef's knife dipped in hot water and wiped dry between cuts. The hot blade glides through frozen layers without cracking the drip. Serve straight from the freezer — a 5-minute rest on the counter softens it just enough for easy eating without melting the structure.</p>

<h2>Common Mistakes and Fixes</h2>
<h3>Drip runs off completely</h3>
<p>Your ganache was too warm. Let it cool to 90°F and test on a chilled plate first.</p>
<h3>Layers look muddy</h3>
<p>The ice cream was too soft when spread. Chill each layer fully before adding the next.</p>
<h3>Crust crumbles when slicing</h3>
<p>Press the crust more firmly and freeze it longer. Use melted butter that's still warm when mixing with crumbs.</p>

<p>This drip-decorated ice cream cake is a technique worth mastering. Once you get the temperature right, you can swap flavors, add fillings, or change the topping. The method stays the same. And the result is always a showstopper.</p>
