<p>The smell of peaches bubbling with cinnamon and butter in a hot oven—that's summer in a dish. This easy peach crumble is exactly that: juicy, sweet fruit under a blanket of crisp, buttery oat topping. No complicated steps, no fancy equipment. Just ripe peaches, a handful of pantry staples, and about an hour of your time.</p><p>The key to a great crumble is the ratio. I keep the topping at roughly equal parts flour, oats, and brown sugar by volume, with enough cold butter to bind it into clumps. That gives you a topping that stays crunchy even after the fruit releases its juices. For the peaches, a light coating of cornstarch and sugar draws out just enough liquid to create a syrupy filling without making it runny.</p><h2>Choosing and Prepping the Peaches</h2><p>For the best flavor, use peaches that are ripe but still firm—they should yield slightly to gentle pressure and smell fragrant at the stem. If your peaches are underripe, let them sit on the counter for a day or two. Overripe peaches will break down too much during baking and make the filling mushy.</p><p>Slice the peaches about ½-inch thick. Thicker slices hold their shape better; thinner slices will melt into the filling. I leave the skins on for texture and color, but you can peel them if you prefer (see FAQ for a quick method). Toss the slices with sugar, lemon juice, cornstarch, and spices. The lemon juice brightens the fruit and helps the cornstarch thicken the juices.</p><h2>The Crumble Topping: Technique Matters</h2><p>This is where texture happens. Combine the dry ingredients—flour, oats, brown sugar, cinnamon, salt. Then add cold butter cut into small cubes. Use a pastry cutter, two forks, or your fingertips to work the butter into the dry mix. Stop when the mixture looks like coarse meal with some pea-sized butter pieces. Those little butter bits are what create flaky, crunchy clusters as the butter melts in the oven.</p><p>One common mistake: overworking the butter until it's fully incorporated. You want distinct pieces of butter throughout. If the mixture starts to look like a uniform dough, you've gone too far. Pop it in the fridge for 5 minutes to re-chill if your hands are warm.</p><h2>Assembling and Baking</h2><p>Pour the peach filling into an 8x8-inch baking dish—glass or ceramic works best because they heat evenly. Spread the crumble topping evenly over the fruit. Don't press it down; leave it loose so air can circulate and create crispness.</p><p>Bake at 375°F (190°C) for 30-35 minutes. You'll know it's done when the filling bubbles thickly around the edges and the topping is deep golden brown. If the topping browns too fast, tent loosely with foil after 20 minutes. Let the crumble cool on a wire rack for at least 15 minutes before serving. This rest time lets the filling set so it doesn't run across the plate.</p><h2>Serving and Storing</h2><p>Serve warm, on its own or with a scoop of vanilla ice cream, a drizzle of heavy cream, or a dollop of whipped cream. The contrast of hot fruit and cold cream is classic for a reason.</p><p>Leftovers keep covered in the fridge for up to 3 days. To reheat, warm individual portions in a 350°F oven for about 10 minutes—the microwave will make the topping soggy. You can also reheat the whole dish, covered, in a 300°F oven for 15-20 minutes.</p><h2>Substitutions and Variations</h2><p>This easy peach crumble is flexible. Swap the peaches for nectarines, plums, or a mix of stone fruits. Add a handful of fresh blueberries or raspberries to the filling for extra color and tang. For a gluten-free version, use a certified gluten-free 1-to-1 flour blend (the oats should be certified gluten-free too). For dairy-free, substitute cold coconut oil or a plant-based butter—just keep it cold.</p><p>If you're watching sugar, you can reduce the granulated sugar in the filling by half if your peaches are very sweet. The brown sugar in the topping adds both sweetness and structure, so I don't recommend reducing it.</p><p><strong>Takeaway:</strong> This easy peach crumble is the dessert you make when you want something impressive but effortless. The technique is simple, the ingredients are honest, and the result is a warm, fruity, crunchy bowl of comfort. Make it once, and you'll have the ratio memorized.</p>
