<p>The first time I made chia pudding, it was a sad, soupy mess. The chia seeds had all floated to the top and the bottom was just thin milk. I learned the hard way: the whisk-twice method changes everything. Now I make this peach chia pudding all summer long, when the peaches are so ripe they practically drip juice when you slice them. It’s a no-cook breakfast that feels like dessert, but with enough protein and fiber to keep you full until lunch.</p>

<h2>Why This Ratio Works</h2>
<p>I keep the ratio at 1 part chia seeds to 4 parts liquid. That’s 1/4 cup chia to 1 cup milk. This gives a pudding that’s thick enough to hold its shape on a spoon but still creamy, not gluey. If you use a thinner milk like unsweetened almond, stick with 1:4. For richer milks like full-fat coconut or oat, you can go to 1:3.5 for a denser texture. The key is the second whisk. After the initial mix, let it sit for five minutes. The seeds start to swell and form a gel. Whisk again and you break up any clumps that have formed at the bottom. This step is non-negotiable for a smooth, uniform pudding.</p>

<h2>Choosing the Peaches</h2>
<p>Ripe peaches are everything here. Look for ones that give slightly when you press near the stem and smell fragrant. An underripe peach will be hard and sour, and it won’t soften in the cold pudding. If your peaches are just shy of ripe, let them sit on the counter for a day or two. A splash of lemon juice keeps the diced fruit from turning brown and adds a subtle brightness that balances the sweetness.</p>

<h2>Step-by-Step Method</h2>
<h3>Mix the Base</h3>
<p>In a small bowl, combine the chia seeds, milk, sweetener, vanilla, and a pinch of salt. Whisk until no clumps remain. Let it rest for five minutes. Whisk again. This is the moment that separates good pudding from great pudding.</p>

<h3>Chill</h3>
<p>Cover the bowl and refrigerate for at least four hours. Overnight is ideal. The pudding will be fully set and cold. If you’re in a hurry, you can eat it after two hours, but it will be looser. I prefer the overnight texture.</p>

<h3>Prep the Fruit</h3>
<p>While the pudding sets, wash and dice the peach into small cubes, about 1/2 inch. Toss with lemon juice if using. Keep the peach in the fridge until you’re ready to serve. This prevents browning and keeps it crisp.</p>

<h3>Layer and Serve</h3>
<p>Spoon the chilled pudding into jars or bowls. Top with the diced peaches. Serve immediately. The contrast between the cool, creamy pudding and the juicy, sweet peaches is the whole point.</p>

<h2>Common Mistakes to Avoid</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Not whisking twice:</strong> The seeds settle quickly. One whisk isn’t enough. The second whisk, after a five-minute rest, ensures even hydration.</li>
<li><strong>Using unripe peaches:</strong> Hard, tart peaches won’t soften in the fridge. Taste one before you commit.</li>
<li><strong>Adding fruit too early:</strong> If you mix the peaches into the pudding, they release water and make the pudding watery. Always layer them on top just before eating.</li>
<li><strong>Skipping the salt:</strong> A tiny pinch of salt enhances all the flavors and keeps the pudding from tasting flat.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Variations</h2>
<p>This base works with any stone fruit. Try nectarines, plums, or apricots. In the winter, use thawed frozen peaches or even diced mango. For a crunchier topping, add a sprinkle of toasted sliced almonds or granola just before serving. If you want a sweeter pudding, increase the maple syrup to 1 1/2 tablespoons, but taste first — ripe peaches bring their own sweetness.</p>

<h2>Storage and Make-Ahead Tips</h2>
<p>Make the pudding base up to five days ahead. Store it in a covered container in the fridge. When you’re ready to eat, give it a stir, then add fresh fruit. The peach topping is best added the day you serve it. If you have leftover pudding without fruit, it keeps well. Do not freeze chia pudding — the texture becomes grainy when thawed.</p>

<p><strong>Takeaway:</strong> Peach chia pudding is a simple, flexible breakfast that comes together in ten minutes of active time. The trick is the whisk-twice method and using ripe, flavorful fruit. Once you get the ratio down, you can make it with any fruit you have on hand. It’s the kind of recipe that makes a busy morning feel a little bit special.</p>
