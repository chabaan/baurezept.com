<p>There's something about the combination of dark chocolate and sea salt that instantly transports me to a sunny beach—the crisp edge of a wave, the soft sand underfoot, a warm breeze carrying the scent of salt and something sweet. These Chocolate Chunk Beach Cookies capture that feeling in every bite. The edges are golden and crisp, the centers are soft and chewy, and each cookie is studded with irregular chunks of dark chocolate that melt into glossy pools. A final sprinkle of flaky sea salt gives them that unmistakable sweet-salty finish.</p>
<figure><img src="https://cdn.midjourney.com/d497bf58-a514-4e42-82fd-65ff5d80781e/0_2.png" alt="Dark chocolate chunks being folded into cookie dough in a mixing bowl" /></figure>


<h2>My Salt-to-Butter Ratio</h2>
<p>The key to these cookies isn't just the chocolate—it's the salt. I use 1 1/2 teaspoons of flaky sea salt in the dough itself, plus a pinch on top after baking. That might sound like a lot, but flaky salt has less density than fine table salt, so you get a gentle salinity without overwhelming the sweetness. The butter I use is unsalted, so I control the salt level precisely. If you only have salted butter on hand, reduce the added salt to 1 teaspoon.</p>

<h2>Why Chocolate Chunks, Not Chips</h2>
<p>Chocolate chips are designed to hold their shape, which is fine for some cookies. But for these beach cookies, I want chocolate that pools and cracks as you bite into it. I buy a good-quality dark chocolate bar (60-70% cacao) and chop it into irregular 1/2-inch chunks. Some pieces will be smaller, some larger—that variety creates texture. If you only have chips, use them, but know you'll miss those melty pockets.</p>

<h2>The Technique for a Soft Center and Crisp Edge</h2>
<p>Two things give these cookies their signature texture: the ratio of brown to granulated sugar, and a short chill. I use equal amounts of brown and white sugar by weight. Brown sugar adds moisture and chewiness from its molasses content; white sugar helps the cookies spread and crisp. The 30-minute chill in the fridge solidifies the butter so the cookies don't flatten into puddles. If you skip the chill, you'll get thin, crispy cookies—still good, but not what we're after here.</p>

<h3>Don't Overmix the Dough</h3>
<p>When you add the flour mixture, mix on low speed just until you no longer see streaks of flour. Overmixing develops the gluten, which makes cookies tough. I use a rubber spatula for the final stir to fold in any remaining flour from the bottom of the bowl.</p>

<h2>Baking for the Perfect Doneness</h2>
<p>Preheat your oven to 375°F (190°C). Line your baking sheets with parchment paper—no greasing needed. Scoop the dough into 1.5-tablespoon rounds (a cookie scoop makes this easy) and place them 2 inches apart. Flatten the tops slightly with your palm so they bake evenly.</p>
<p>Bake for 11 to 13 minutes. The edges should be deep golden brown, but the centers will still look puffy and soft—almost underdone. That's exactly what you want. The residual heat from the pan will finish cooking the centers as they cool. If you wait until the centers look fully set, you'll have dry cookies.</p>

<h3>The Final Salt Sprinkle</h3>
<p>As soon as the cookies come out of the oven, sprinkle a tiny pinch of flaky sea salt over each one. This is the moment that makes them "beach cookies." The salt sticks to the warm surface and creates that savory-sweet contrast that keeps people reaching for another.</p>

<h2>Common Mistakes to Avoid</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Skipping the chill.</strong> Warm dough spreads too much. Even 20 minutes makes a difference.</li>
<li><strong>Using fine salt for the finish.</strong> Fine salt dissolves and tastes harsh. Flaky sea salt stays visible and gives a gentle crunch.</li>
<li><strong>Overbaking.</strong> Pull the cookies when the centers are still soft. They will set up as they cool on the sheet.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Storing and Serving</h2>
<p>These cookies keep well in an airtight container at room temperature for up to 5 days. If they start to dry out, add a slice of bread to the container—it releases moisture and softens the cookies overnight. For a party spread, arrange them on a wooden board with fresh berries and a bowl of cold milk. They also freeze beautifully: bake from frozen, adding 1-2 minutes to the bake time.</p>

<h2>Takeaway</h2>
<p>Chocolate Chunk Beach Cookies are about balance—crisp and soft, sweet and salty, simple and special. The technique is straightforward, but the small details (the salt ratio, the chunk size, the chill time) make all the difference. Bake a batch, close your eyes, and you'll almost hear the waves.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<h3>Why do you use flaky sea salt instead of regular salt?</h3>
<p>Flaky sea salt has a lower density and larger crystals, so you get bursts of salty crunch without making the dough overly salty. The finishing salt on top also creates that sweet-salty contrast that defines the beach cookie flavor.</p>
<h3>Can I use chocolate chips instead of chocolate chunks?</h3>
<p>Yes, but chunks give you those irregular, melty pools of chocolate in each bite. If using chips, choose a high-quality brand with at least 60% cacao for the best flavor.</p>
<h3>Why chill the dough for 30 minutes?</h3>
<p>Chilling solidifies the butter so the cookies spread less in the oven, giving you a thicker, chewier center. It also allows the flour to fully hydrate and the flavors to meld.</p>
<h3>How do I know when the cookies are done baking?</h3>
<p>Look for golden-brown edges with still-soft, puffy centers. The cookies will continue to set as they cool on the sheet. Overbaking yields dry, crunchy cookies — remove them when the centers look slightly underdone.</p>
<h3>Can I freeze the dough?</h3>
<p>Absolutely. Scoop the dough into balls, freeze on a sheet until solid, then transfer to a freezer bag. Bake from frozen, adding 1-2 extra minutes to the bake time.</p>
